# PhysioPath: the physiotherapist's assistant during the session

*Working name*

**Concept summary:** At the start of a session the physiotherapist speaks the day's exercises into PhysioPath. The patient then follows them on a phone or tablet, one exercise at a time, while the physio sees every patient in the room on one screen. At the end, the same session becomes the patient's home program and the clinical note. Built first for physiotherapists in Cyprus and Greece, in Greek.

---

## 1. The problem

**During the session, the physio is the bottleneck.** In many clinics a physiotherapist works with two or three patients at once: one on the bed, one on exercises, one waiting for the next instruction. Each time a patient finishes a set, they stop and wait, or call the physio over. The physio keeps walking back to repeat sets, repetitions and the next exercise. Paid minutes are lost on both sides, the physio is constantly interrupted, and patients feel left alone.

**After the session, the paperwork follows.** Clinical notes and written instructions for the patient take extra unpaid time. Many small clinics still rely on paper, a whiteboard or WhatsApp, because existing software is expensive, complicated or not in Greek.

In short: the physio cannot be in three places at once, and nothing in the room keeps the patient going without them.

## 2. The solution

Think of PhysioPath as a second pair of hands for the physio during the session.

1. **Speak the plan (30 seconds).** While the patient warms up, the physio says, for example: *"Γιώργος, ώμος, εβδομάδα 3. Pendulum ένα λεπτό, wall slides 3×10, isometric external rotation 10 δευτερόλεπτα × 8."*
2. **The patient follows it on a screen.** On their own phone (by scanning a QR code) or on a clinic tablet at the station, the patient sees one exercise at a time: the name, sets and repetitions, a hold countdown, and a big "Τελείωσα το σετ" button. When they finish, the next exercise appears on its own.
3. **The physio sees the whole room.** A room board shows each patient's progress, who has finished and who needs the physio. Patients can call the physio from the screen ("Απορία" or "Πόνος") instead of waiting or shouting across the room.
4. **The session ends itself.** With one tap, the session becomes the patient's home program (a simple link) and a clinical note in professional form.

The physiotherapist reviews everything before the patient sees it. **The physio decides, the AI writes.**

## 3. Who it is for, and who pays

The physiotherapist or the clinic pays a monthly subscription. Patients use it for free.

| Segment | Why it matters to them |
|---|---|
| **Sports and calisthenics clinics** | Exercise-heavy sessions with many exercises per patient: the most value from a session screen. |
| **Solo physiotherapists** | One person doing everything; every interruption costs them directly. Quick decisions, own budget. |
| **Multi-physio clinics (GeSY)** | Busy rooms with many patients in parallel; the room board and less waiting matter most. A bigger plan per clinic. |

Interviews will show which segment to serve first.

## 4. Why physios will pay

- **More patients handled well per hour.** Fewer interruptions and less waiting make parallel work smooth instead of stressful.
- **Fewer drop-outs.** Patients who feel guided and see their progress complete more of their treatment, which means more sessions.
- **A modern, professional image** compared with a sheet on the wall.
- **Less paperwork** as a free by-product: the home program and the clinical note come from the session that already happened.

*Example to check in interviews: if the tool saves ten minutes of interruptions a day, or keeps one patient a week from dropping out, it pays for itself many times over.*

## 5. Why now

- AI can now understand spoken Greek and turn it into structured text reliably and at low cost.
- Phones are everywhere, and a web link needs no app download.
- Most tools on the market are English-first and priced for large organisations. Small practices in Cyprus and Greece are underserved.

## 6. Business model

- **Monthly subscription per physiotherapist**, and a **clinic plan** for several physios and a shared room screen.
- Patients use it for free, through a link or a clinic tablet.
- *Exact pricing will be decided after conversations with physiotherapists.*

## 7. Competition and how we are different

Exercise-program platforms with video libraries and AI note-taking tools already exist, which shows that clinicians pay for help. They are built for sending programs home or writing notes, not for running the session itself.

- **In-session first.** Built for the moment the patient is in the room, not only for afterwards.
- **Voice first and Greek first.** The physio speaks for 30 seconds in Greek, mixing in English terms as they normally do.
- **The physio's own plan.** Exactly what the physio said, not a generic template.
- **Simple and affordable.** For solo physios and small clinics, with local, in-person support.

## 8. Responsibility, privacy and safety

- **GDPR compliance:** patient consent, secure storage, servers within the EU.
- **Human approval:** nothing the AI writes reaches a patient without the physiotherapist reviewing it.
- **A documentation and organisation tool, not a medical one:** the AI only records and organises what the physio decided. It does not diagnose, suggest treatments or advise patients. The "call the physio" button only passes the message on. This keeps patients safe and keeps the product clear of medical device rules in its first stages.

## 9. First version (demo)

1. The physio speaks the day's plan in Greek.
2. The AI turns it into today's exercises; the physio checks them and starts the session.
3. The patient follows the exercises on their phone (QR) or a tablet; the physio watches the room board.
4. At the end, one tap creates the home program link and the clinical note.

Reminders, exercise videos, progress over time and a clinic dashboard come only after real physiotherapists use the first version and tell us what they need.
