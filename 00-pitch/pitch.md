# PhysioPath: speak once, the note and the home program are done

*Working name*

**Concept summary:** At the end of a session the physiotherapist speaks the patient's home exercises into PhysioPath for 30 seconds, the way they already explain them to the patient. PhysioPath turns that into the patient's home program (a simple link) and the clinical note, ready for GeSY. The physio checks both before anything is used. Built first for physiotherapists in Cyprus and Greece, in Greek.

---

## 1. The problem

**After every session, the paperwork follows.** GeSY requires a clinical note for every paid session: the service given, the treatment plan and the patient's progress. Physios write it between patients, at the end of the day or at home. It's unpaid time.

**Home exercises get lost.** Most patients get a paper sheet, a drawing or a WhatsApp message, or just remember what they heard. Around 70% of patients don't do their home exercises as prescribed (US study). Many small clinics still rely on paper or WhatsApp, because existing software is expensive, complicated or not in Greek.

In short: the physio explains everything once, out loud, then has to write it twice, once for GeSY and once for the patient.

## 2. The solution

1. **Speak (30 s).** At the end of the session the physio explains the home exercises to the patient, with PhysioPath listening: *"Γιώργος, ώμος, εβδομάδα 3. Pendulum ένα λεπτό, wall slides 3×10, isometric external rotation 10 δευτερόλεπτα × 8."*
2. **Check.** The physio sees the home program and the clinical note, with missing details (no reps, no frequency) highlighted. They edit and approve.
3. **The patient gets a link.** One exercise per screen, large text, in plain Greek. No app, no login.
4. **The note is ready.** In the format GeSY asks for, to copy into the GeSY portal.

The physio stays with the patient the whole session. **The physio decides, the AI writes.**

## 3. Who it is for, and who pays

The physiotherapist or the clinic pays a monthly subscription. Patients use it for free.

| Segment | Why it matters to them |
|---|---|
| **GeSY physiotherapists (solo and small clinics)** | A required note after every paid session: the most repeated paperwork of the day. Quick decisions, own budget. |
| **Sports and calisthenics clinics** | Many exercises per patient, so a clear home program matters most. |
| **Multi-physio clinics** | The same note format and home program for every physio; one clinic plan. |

Interviews will show which segment to serve first.

## 4. Why physios will pay

- **Less paperwork.** The note is done before the patient leaves, not at home at night.
- **Fewer drop-outs.** Patients who get a clear program and do their exercises complete more of their treatment, which means more sessions.
- **A modern, professional image** compared with a photocopied sheet or a WhatsApp message.

*Example to check in interviews: if each note takes 5 minutes today and 1 minute with PhysioPath, a physio with 10 sessions a day gets back 40 minutes every day.*

## 5. Why now

- AI can now understand spoken Greek and turn it into structured text reliably and at low cost.
- Phones are everywhere, and a web link needs no app download.
- Most tools on the market are English-first and priced for large organisations. Small practices in Cyprus and Greece are underserved.

## 6. Business model

- **Monthly subscription per physiotherapist**, and a **clinic plan** for several physios.
- Patients use it for free, through a link.
- *Exact pricing will be decided after conversations with physiotherapists.*

## 7. Competition and how we are different

Exercise-program platforms and AI note-taking tools already exist, which shows that clinicians pay for help. None of them covers both jobs in Greek:

- **Exercise-program platforms** (Physitrack, Medbridge, SimpleSet and others) mean building programs by clicking through a video library. Physitrack, the market leader, is not in Greek.
- **AI scribes** (Physitrack's new one, Heidi) write notes, are English-first and don't produce a home program.
- **Greek clinic software** (4physio, iStations) handles appointments and records, with no AI and nothing for the patient.

How we are different:

- **One voice note, two outputs.** The home program and the clinical note come from the same 30 seconds.
- **Voice first and Greek first.** The physio speaks in Greek, mixing in English terms as they normally do.
- **Shaped for GeSY.** The note follows what GeSY asks for.
- **The physio's own plan.** Exactly what the physio said, not a generic template.
- **Simple and affordable.** For solo physios and small clinics, with local, in-person support.

## 8. Responsibility, privacy and safety

- **GDPR compliance:** patient consent, secure storage, servers within the EU.
- **Human approval:** nothing the AI writes reaches a patient without the physiotherapist reviewing it.
- **A documentation tool, not a medical one:** the AI only records and organises what the physio decided. It does not diagnose, suggest treatments or advise patients. This keeps patients safe and keeps the product clear of medical device rules in its first stages.

## 9. First version (demo)

1. At the end of the session, the physio speaks the home exercises in Greek.
2. The AI turns them into the home program and the clinical note; missing details are highlighted.
3. The physio checks, edits and approves.
4. The patient gets the program as a link; the note is ready to copy.

Reminders, exercise videos, progress over time and a clinic dashboard come only after real physiotherapists use the first version and tell us what they need.

*Later, maybe:* a session mode (the patient follows exercises on a screen, the physio sees a room board) for GeSY group exercise (PHYS04: 2–4 neuro or scoliosis patients), private group classes or sports rehab, where working with several patients at once is normal and allowed.

## 10. Plan for the next months

1. **Listen:** interview 5 to 10 physiotherapists in Limassol about how they write notes and give home exercises today, GeSY and private.
2. **Observe:** with patient permission, sit in on real sessions and time how long notes and home-exercise explanations take, and when notes get written.
3. **Prototype:** test the demo with one or two friendly physios, using fictional patients first.
4. **Pilot:** a small free pilot with a few clinics; measure time per note, how much physios edit, and patient feedback.
5. **Launch:** start charging once physiotherapists confirm they would pay for it.
