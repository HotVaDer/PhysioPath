## What the research shows

1. **Nobody runs the in-room session.** Every exercise platform (Physitrack, Medbridge, Wibbi, SimpleSet) is built for *after* the session: build a program, send it home, track adherence. None found has a room board, live set progress across several patients, or a "call the physio" button. That is PhysioPath's clearest gap.
2. **No product combines Greek, voice and exercises.** The Greek products (4physio, iStations) only manage the clinic. The voice AI tools (Physitrack's scribe, Heidi) are English-first and make notes, not patient sessions. Curify is Greek and voice-driven, but it handles admin.
3. **All the library platforms work the same way:** search a video library and click exercises together. PhysioPath's "speak for 30 seconds" flow is a different input, not a smaller library.
4. **Patient access has friction.** Physitrack needs a code + birth year or an app. Older patients and older Android phones are a known pain point. A QR code with no login is a real advantage; prove it with older patients.
5. **The prices give us a ceiling.** Library platforms cost about €11–30 per physio per month; an AI EMR with a scribe costs about €55–75. A solo physio in Cyprus will compare us with the low end, and with "free" (paper).
6. **No GeSY software turned up.** Nothing found handles GeSY referrals or claims. This is a possible later wedge, but only if interviews show it hurts.

## Threats

- **Physitrack adds Greek dictation.** It has a scribe and says non-English dictation is coming. Voice + Greek alone is not a lasting advantage; the live room workflow and local relationships are harder to copy.
- **A general scribe (Heidi and others) adds Greek well.** That would cover the clinical-note part, so the note should stay a by-product, not the main selling point.
- **Low-cost platforms (SimpleSet, Rehab My Patient) translate into Greek.** They would compete on home programs, not on the session.
