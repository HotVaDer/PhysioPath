# Physiotherapist Interview Guide

Goal: understand how physios handle notes and home exercises **today**, before showing any solution. Ask about past behaviour, not opinions about a future product.

Length: 20–30 min. Ask for permission before taking notes or recording.

## Intro (2 min)

- Who I am, why I'm asking (my own rehab after bicep surgery).
- "I'm not selling anything. I want to understand how your work happens today."

## About them (3 min)

1. How long have you been practising? Solo or in a clinic? How many physios work there?
2. How many patients do you see on a typical day?
3. What kind of patients mostly (sports, post-surgery, elderly, neuro…)?

## Clinical notes (7 min)

4. Walk me through what happens after a session ends. What do you write, and where?
5. When do you write your notes? During the session, between patients, at the end of the day, at home?
6. How long does it take you per patient? Per week?
7. What software or tools do you use? What do you like / dislike about them? What do you pay?
8. Who reads your notes (you, colleagues, insurance, GESY, doctors)? Is there a required format?

## Home exercises (8 min)

9. Tell me about the last patient you gave home exercises to. How did you give them the instructions?
10. Paper, drawings, photos, WhatsApp, an app? Why that way?
11. How do you know whether patients actually do the exercises? How often do they?
12. What goes wrong most often? (wrong technique, forget, give up, lose the paper…)
13. Have you tried any app for this? Why did you keep it / stop?

## Money and priorities (5 min)

14. If you could get rid of one annoying part of your day, what would it be?
15. Have you ever paid for a tool to save time? Which one, how much?
16. Who decides on buying software in your clinic?

## Wrap up (2 min)

17. Can I come and observe a session sometime (with patient permission)?
18. Who else should I talk to?
19. Can I contact you again when I have something to show?

## Things to watch for

- Exact words they use for the problem (useful for marketing copy later).
- Workarounds they have built themselves = strong signal of real pain.
- Avoid leading questions like "Would you use an app that…?"
